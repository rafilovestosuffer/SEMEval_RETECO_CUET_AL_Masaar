# Stop tagging, stop fusing BM25, start fusing views

**Three of the four levers currently written into your planning documents are wrong, and the replacement is cheaper than any of them.** The "+8.0 from temporal intent tagging" in TEMPO Table 5 is an oracle condition built from the benchmark's own GPT-4o gold annotations, it is undocumented and absent from the public repo, and it is **−1.8 for DiVeR specifically** while averaging about **−0.6 across the eleven non-ReasonIR retrievers** ([TEMPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09523v1)) — so for a Diver-Retriever first stage it is a negative lever, not a +8. Hybrid BM25+dense interpolation at weight 0.5 is reported by two independent groups to **cost 2–6 nDCG@10 points** once the dense arm clears roughly 20 BRIGHT nDCG@10 ([arXiv 2604.03676](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03676v1); [arXiv 2509.02558](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02558)), and the Waterloo reproduction finds **Diver 29.1 → 27.2 fused**, contradicting DIVER's own unablated +8.3 claim. RRF is the wrong operator in your regime because with **77.7% of queries scoring exactly 0.0** it hands a junk list's rank-1 document the same 1/61 credit as the dense arm's rank-1 ([Bruch et al., TOIS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11934)). And the assumed ~60% recall@100 ceiling does not exist in any source: the only absolute BRIGHT recall@100 table gives **BM25 35.7, dense ~39.4, hybrid ~45.1** on corpora 4–200× smaller than yours ([EnrichIndex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03598)), so your measured **0.268 at 1.65M documents is in line, not broken**. What replaces all of it: **equal-weight fusion of offline-generated reasoning views, measured on TEMPO itself at 0.265 → 0.284 with no learning whatsoever**, with a learned gate adding only **~+0.005 at matched K** ([arXiv 2608.08940](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08940v1)).

## The +8.0 is one unexplained cell in a table whose other eleven rows go the wrong way

TEMPO's Table 5 is a query-variant ablation with four conditions — Baseline, Strip, Temporal-Only, and **Normalized** (explicit temporal intent tags added) — and the Normalized column moves ReasonIR from 27.3 to **35.3**, while BM25 goes 10.8 → 10.9, SBERT −1.4, E5 −1.3, Rader −2.6 and **DiVeR 32.0 → 30.2** ([TEMPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09523v1)). The paper's own gloss concedes the conditionality: ReasonIR is best "**when provided structured temporal metadata**." That metadata is the benchmark's GPT-4o annotation layer — Appendix B.2 describes GPT-4o emitting a JSON object with temporal intent classification and signals, which is the provenance of the `temporal_intent` fields that ship in `guidance_*.jsonl`. No classifier is described anywhere in the paper, and the public repo ([tempo-bench/Tempo](https://github.com/tempo-bench/Tempo)) ships only `retrievers.py`, `run.py`, `run_step.py`, `temporal_metrics.py` and `run.sh` — **no query-variant construction code at all**. The exact Normalized template, an example pair, and whether any classifier was involved are **[UNVERIFIED]** and could not be confirmed from any source; do not let the system paper state a specific template as TEMPO's.

The mechanism that explains the pattern also predicts your outcome. ReasonIR-8B is explicitly instruction-conditioned, jointly encoding a natural-language instruction with the query ([ReasonIR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.20595v1)), so appended text that reads as an instruction moves it and leaves plain encoders untouched or slightly worse. Diver-Retriever is not in that family. Even in the best case the arithmetic collapses: NTCIR Temporalia's four-way temporal orientation task topped out at **66.33% accuracy** and **0.74 precision** with classical features ([Temporalia-TQIC](https://github.com/filannim/Temporalia-TQIC)), and your label space is worse — measured directly from your local `guidance_train.jsonl`, **N = 1,211 queries across 18 `temporal_intent` values**, long-tailed (`when` 277, `period_definition` 244, `timeline` 243, down to six values with ≤2 examples) and **demonstrably noisy**, with five reasoning-class names leaking into the intent field and 36 empty-string primary classes. Collapsing to six classes (`when / period_definition / timeline / ongoing_status / duration / other`) covers **999/1,211 = 82.5%**; adding an ordering class reaches **89%** at seven classes.

So the honest answer to question (1): **temporal intent tagging is not worth implementing as a query-text treatment.** Build exactly one piece of it — a **binary `is_temporal_query` gate from frozen embeddings plus sklearn logistic regression**, under five GPU-minutes on embeddings you already compute — and use it as a *routing feature*, never as text appended to the query. Skip HeidelTime and SUTime entirely: they reach 86–90 F1 on news prose ([HeidelTime, SemEval-2010](https://dl.acm.org/citation.cfm?id=1859664.1859735); [TempEval-3 comparisons](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.08120)) but degrade on short text, carry a Java dependency onto Kaggle, and TEMPO's Temporal-Only column settles the matter — reducing queries to their temporal content collapses every dense model (E5 30.4 → 14.2, DiVeR 32.0 → 17.7, SBERT 24.9 → 8.7) while BM25 is unchanged. **Time expressions carry almost no standalone retrieval signal on this benchmark.** A 30-line regex gives you the same routing features at zero cost.

One finding from this strand is worth more than the tagging question itself, and it is a bug-class fix rather than an experiment. TEMPO Figure 8 shows LLM reasoning augmentation lifting ReasonIR **+13.7** while **BM25 collapses from 10.8 to 4.3–6.2** as generated text dilutes the lexical signal, against 10.9 for short tags. **Feed the raw query to the sparse arm and the augmented query to the dense arm only.** If your pipeline currently shares one augmented query string across both arms, you are losing roughly five points on the sparse side and it may not surface until submission. Verify this before anything else; it costs zero GPU-hours.

## Step fusion: keep the parent query, normalise the scores, and sweep w

For question (2), the design that the evidence supports is a **normalised weighted sum over the parent query and its steps**, not RRF and not replacement:

```
S(d) = w · s_q(d)  +  (1 − w) · (1/N) Σ_i s_i(d)
```

with per-list **z-normalisation over the top-N and an explicit penalty constant δ for documents absent from a list**, sweeping `w ∈ {1.0, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7, 0.6, 0.5, 0.35, 0.2, 0.0}`.

Three independent decomposition papers converge on additive score aggregation for *complementary* sub-queries. ReDI's direct ablation on BRIGHT StackExchange gives **Sum 38.3 sparse / 23.7 dense, RRF 36.8 / 21.5, Max 35.2 / 22.5, Concat 30.7 / 12.4** ([ReDI](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06544v1)) — sum beats RRF by +1.5 sparse and +2.2 dense, and concatenating text loses 7.6 to 11.3 points. Multi-aspect reviewed-item retrieval finds the arithmetic mean (equivalently equal-weight CombSUM) the winner over six operators including Borda and round-robin ([arXiv 2408.00878](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00878v1)), and the stage-aware study finds Score-Sum optimal among its fusion configurations ([arXiv 2606.08577](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08577)). The RRF-wins evidence comes from a different regime — Benham & Culpepper over *query variations*, where CombMNZ is strongest on mean effectiveness and RRF best on risk ([ADCS 2017](https://rodgerbenham.github.io/bc17-adcs.pdf)), and MMLF over LLM-generated multi-perspective variants ([Findings of NAACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.367/)). **The literature splits cleanly along complementary-versus-substitutable lines, but no paper names or tests that distinction** — which makes stating and ablating it a genuine, unclaimed framing for your system paper. Do not cite MMLF per-dataset numbers or its internal ablation until you read the PDF locally; it failed to parse twice and its "+4% avg, +8% max" is abstract-level only.

Keeping the parent query is what separates every positive result from the one strong negative. The stage-aware study measures decomposition at first stage **costing 1.6–4.3 nDCG@10** (BGE-large worst at −4.3, Qwen3 variants −1.6 to −2.2) and 1–3 absolute Recall@50 points, while the same sub-queries **gain 3.9–7.6 at reranking** (Qwen3-Reranker-8B +5.9) — and it **exclusively tested replacement**. The CIKM'26 view-fusion paper includes the original query as view v₀ and gets its best result at K=3. That mismatch is the whole story: replacing discards global semantic context, augmenting cannot, because **w=1 recovers your 1a baseline exactly**. This makes the parent-weight sweep a monotone-safe experiment — the worst case is a flat curve peaking at w=1, which is a publishable negative that cleanly explains the augment/replace split in the literature.

**And this is where your contribution actually lives.** A continuous parent-versus-steps weight sweep is absent from ReDI, MMLF, the stage-aware study, aspect fusion, and the CIKM'26 gate paper. So is equal-weight fusion at matched K, RRF k-sensitivity for 3–5 lists, the missing-document penalty δ, and the cost of duplicates consuming fused top-10 slots. Four unmeasured cells, all cheap.

Two details decide more than the operator choice. First, **δ is a second, unreported hyperparameter**: with 3–5 lists truncated at top-100, most documents are absent from most lists, so whatever you assign an absent document controls how strongly one step can veto it. Under min-max, absent defaults to 0 — mild. Under z-score, `min_z − δ` — and nobody sweeps δ. The exact constant used in the CIKM'26 paper is **[UNVERIFIED]**. Second, **your top-100 truncation is a censoring mechanism** and it is well below ReDI's top-1k per unit. A document ranked 101 for one step is scored as *absent*, a much harsher statement than "ranked 101." Run depth 100/300/1000 before publishing any Tier-0 number, or your conclusions may be depth artifacts.

On the gating question the notes correct a premise you were carrying. The verified Table 2 values on the SFR backbone are **original query K=1 = 0.265, equal-weight K=5 = 0.284, gated K=5 = 0.289, gated K=3 = 0.297**, and **equal-weight at K=3 is not reported anywhere in the paper**. The headline +0.013 therefore compares gate@K=3 against equal-weight@K=5 — it is partly a K effect, not a gating effect. At matched K the ~1,000-parameter gate buys **+0.005**, which against 1,211 queries is unlikely to separate from seed noise under a paired bootstrap. The more interesting fact in that table is that **K=3 beat K=5 for the gated model: adding views hurt.** That is direct evidence bad sub-queries poison additive fusion, and it argues for **pruning over gating** — a one-parameter "drop the lowest-QPP step" rule before any learned soft gate. Your data gives 3–5 steps with a median of 3, suspiciously close to the observed optimum. Measuring equal-weight@K=3 yourself is the single most decision-relevant missing number in that literature, and it cleanly isolates gating from K.

Duplicates deserve one diagnostic row. No source addresses near-duplicate documents in sub-query fusion. Analytically they do not distort either copy's fused score under CombSUM, RRF or MNZ — the damage is entirely metric-side, where two identical documents at ranks 1–2 consume two of ten nDCG@10 slots and only one is in qrels. With **gold median 3 per query**, that is a large relative loss. **Deduplicate after fusion, before truncating to 10**, never before fusing.

## Fuse BM25 for the pool, not for the score

Question (3) has a two-part answer, and the two notes only appear to disagree because they optimise different objectives.

**For the final nDCG@10 run: do not fuse.** The threshold is stated outright in the BRIGHT cost study — linear fusion with BM25 is "a reliable, zero-cost upgrade for any dense retriever scoring below ~20 nDCG@10 on BRIGHT" and should be avoided above it, because BM25 promotes "lexically similar but semantically irrelevant documents" once the dense model is already effective ([arXiv 2604.03676](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03676v1)). The measured regressions: **GTE-Qwen2 23.3 → 21.3, ReasonIR 24.1 → 21.2**, against gains for weak arms (BGE 13.7 → 17.2, Instructor-L 14.2 → 19.1, SFR-Mistral 18.3 → 23.1). Waterloo reproduces the pattern independently — **Diver 29.1 → 27.2, Reason-Embed-4B 36.9 → 31.1** — and states that "fusing BM25Q with reasoning retrievers generally reduces effectiveness" ([arXiv 2509.02558](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02558)). **DIVER's own +8.3 (28.9 → 37.2) has no weight ablation in the paper and is contradicted in sign by that reproduction; treat it as unreproduced and budget nothing for it.** Your regime is strictly worse than theirs on both sides: BM25 at 9.4 rather than 14.5, dense expected around 30–32. Vogt & Cottrell's conditions for linear combination require high performance from *both* systems, a large overlap of **relevant** documents and a small overlap of non-relevant ones ([Information Retrieval 1999](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1009980820262)) — your 56.6% no-gold-in-top-100 statistic fails the relevant-overlap condition directly.

**The operator, if you fuse at all, is weighted score interpolation with theoretical min-max — never RRF.** Bruch et al. sweep α across the full interval and find convex combination beats RRF in-domain and out-of-domain, converging to near-optimal α on **under 5% of the training data (~350 MS MARCO queries)**, while RRF "disregards the distribution of scores" and nDCG "swings wildly as a function of rrf parameters" ([arXiv 2210.11934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11934)). Their out-of-domain band is **α ≈ 0.6–0.8 on the dense arm even for a healthy sparse arm**; yours is a third as strong again. Normalisation: theoretical min-max (lower bound fixed at 0) is the most robust of the three they compare because it uses one fewer data-dependent statistic. Per-query empirical min-max is the specific thing that breaks here — it rescales pure noise to fill [0,1] and manufactures a spurious 1.0 for the top junk document on 77.7% of queries. **Divide instead by a global high quantile — the 99th percentile of train BM25 scores — which keeps weak queries weak.** That variant is **[UNVERIFIED as a published method]**; it is an extrapolation from Bruch's robustness argument, since no paper studies normalisation under a degenerate arm.

Run the sweep anyway, because it is cheap and it is the evidence that defends the decision: `a ∈ {0, 0.02, 0.05, 0.08, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.30, 0.50}`, resolution concentrated below 0.2. Expect a monotone decline from a=0 or a shallow plateau to a≈0.05–0.10. **A broad inverted-U peaking at 0.5 is a bug signal, not a win** — that shape belongs to a regime where BM25 is competitive ([arXiv 2608.00183](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00183)). Adopt fusion only if CV-best beats a=0 with a paired bootstrap CI excluding zero; under 0.005 is not worth the pipeline complexity. Skip the learned per-query router: the one study that evaluated routers under grouped cross-validation found **+21.8% oracle headroom and zero realised gain** from all three (heuristic −0.0161, random forest −0.00004, ridge +0.0022 with a CI spanning zero). If anything, use a single-threshold unsupervised gate on top-1 BM25 score and abandon on a null.

**For the candidate pool feeding a reranker, the calculus flips**, and this is where the two notes reconcile. The largest recall@100 delta anywhere in this review belongs to hybrid: **~45.1 versus BM25 35.7 and dense ~39.4** on BRIGHT ([EnrichIndex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03598)) — coverage, not scoring. A **union** of the two top-k pools requires no cross-system score calibration and cannot suppress a document that only one system found, whereas a weighted blend can. So: union for the pool, `a=0` for the ranking. The number that decides whether BM25 belongs in the pool at all is **marginal recall@100 — gold documents BM25 finds that dense misses, as a fraction of all gold.** If it is near zero, no α and no union can help, and BM25 leaves the pipeline entirely. Measure that before anything else in Phase 4.

## What actually raises first-stage recall on 30 GPU-hours a week

Question (4), and the answer is severe: **everything that scales with 1.65M documents is unaffordable, unevidenced on reasoning retrieval, or legally ambiguous.** Everything that scales with 1,730 queries is cheap, measured, and rule-safe.

Document expansion is the clearest casualty. Original doc2query moves MS MARCO Recall@1000 from **85.3 to 89.3** ([arXiv 1904.08375](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.08375)) — +4.0 points at depth 1000 on an easy lexical benchmark with no reasoning-benchmark evidence at all, and docT5query is **~140× more expensive than TILDE** ([SPRINT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10488)), putting a full pass at order **50–150 T4-hours [UNVERIFIED — derived from the cost ratio, not measured]**, i.e. two to five weeks of your entire quota. EnrichIndex is the one document-side method with real reasoning-retrieval numbers — offline GPT-4o-mini generation of a purpose statement, a jargon-free summary and **20 QA pairs** per document, giving **+5.57 recall@100 for dense but only +1.1 for BM25** on BRIGHT — and it is flatly infeasible over 1.65M documents here. Both carry a **rule risk that must be resolved in writing before any GPU-hours are spent**: the expansions derive from the official corpus, but they are LLM-generated and materially change the index. Ask the organizers; do not build the primary system on it. Late interaction is a third dead end for a different reason — ColBERTv2's index runs **16 GiB at 1 bit/dim on ~8.8M MS MARCO passages** ([arXiv 2112.01488](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.01488)) and PLAID's virtue is latency (35–100 ms on GPU at 140M passages, [arXiv 2205.09707](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.09707)), which is irrelevant to an offline evaluation. **No ColBERT or PLAID result exists on BRIGHT, TEMPO or any reasoning benchmark.**

What is left is the query side, and it is strong. BRIGHT's own authors report chain-of-thought query reasoning lifting **sparse models by up to 12.2 nDCG points** ([BRIGHT](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12883v4)) — the raw query is the bottleneck, not the index. DIVER's largest documented retriever gain is query expansion, **28.9 → 33.9 standalone**, larger than its disputed BM25 interpolation delta ([DIVER](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07995)). And the organizers' own CIKM'26 method is measured **on TEMPO, your benchmark, with your metric**: views named change-over-time, compare-baseline and event-ordering plus the original query, **"produced once offline by GPT-4o using a single fixed prompt, with no access to relevance labels,"** with **"The LLM is never queried at retrieval time"** ([arXiv 2608.08940](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08940v1)). Gains span **Contriever 19.6 → 25.9, E5 27.1 → 30.3, SFR 26.5 → 29.7**, p<0.001 over 1,730 queries, and **"the improvement is largest for the weakest backbones"** — which matters, because you are running the 0.6B. The exact view-generation prompt is **[UNVERIFIED]**, not retrievable from the sources; you will write your own.

That last paper is also your legal blueprint. An offline, label-free reformulation of *test* queries is the organizers' own published method, reading no gold guidance and adding nothing to the index. Note one caveat the notes flag: whether views were generated for the test split or only train is **[UNVERIFIED]** from the extracts obtained, as is whether the numbers cover TEMPO's full 1,730 queries.

Finally, retrieve deep and rerank shallow. Retrieving 1000 instead of 100 is a k-parameter and free; reranking cost scales linearly, **1,730 × 1000 = 1.73M pairs versus 173K at depth 100**, which is the difference between roughly 1–2 and 10–20 T4-hours **[UNVERIFIED — derived from pair counts]**. Published maximum-gain reranking depth sits between **100 and 150** ([Elastic Search Labs](https://www.elastic.co/search-labs/blog/elastic-semantic-reranker-part-3)), and pipelines reranking 100 documents can often be matched by reranking **50** ([arXiv 2601.14224](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14224v2)). **Measure recall@1000 on train this week** — it costs nothing and tells you exactly how much headroom pool expansion can buy. If recall@1000 is ~0.45 against recall@100 of 0.27, expansion has a target; if it is only ~0.32, BM25 is fundamentally blind on those queries and only a different retriever helps.

## Conclusion

The through-line is that every lever in this design space that looked large was measured on a model unlike yours, and every lever that transfers is small, cheap and query-side. The +8.0 and the +8.3 are the same kind of error in different clothes: a single unablated table cell generalised into a plan. What survives replication is unglamorous — **+0.019 from equal-weight view fusion with no learning at all**, a few points from keeping the parent query in the fusion set rather than replacing it, and a handful of points saved by *not* doing things (not sharing augmented text with BM25, not interpolating a degenerate sparse arm, not using RRF where 77.7% of one arm's queries are exactly zero). Under a 30-hour weekly quota, the avoided losses are worth more than the pursued gains.

That reframes the paper. You are not going to out-engineer a well-funded team on rank, and you do not need to: the contribution surface here is unusually wide precisely because the field has been reporting headline deltas without the ablations underneath them. **The parent-versus-steps weight sweep, equal-weight fusion at matched K, RRF k-sensitivity at 3–5 lists, the missing-document penalty δ, and the duplicate cost in a fused top-10 are all absent from every paper reviewed** — and the first is monotone-safe, meaning it produces a reportable result whether it works or not. A system paper that establishes the first recall curve on TEMPO, isolates gating from K in the organizers' own method, and demonstrates that BM25 fusion inverts below the published ~20 nDCG threshold is a better artifact than one that placed three ranks higher by copying a 0.5.
