# Point the reranker at the steps

**The single highest-value next improvement is to run the ReasonRank-7B reranker you already have on the 1b step runs, which currently get no reranking at all.** Reranking is the only stage in your pipeline that has delivered more than one nDCG@10 point: **+0.0508 on train (896 queries) and +0.0673 on dev**, against +0.0115 / +0.0080 for step fusion ([ledger](../results/ledger.csv)). Sub-track 1b is ranked separately and sits at **dev 0.3147 with plain dense step runs**, so a gain of even half the 1a reranking effect (+0.03) on 1b is worth more than every remaining 1a lever combined. Every 1a candidate in the notes (temporal query views, score interpolation, GroupRank-7B, deeper reranking, a 4B first stage, temporal-specific methods) comes out at **roughly +0.00 to +0.02 final nDCG@10** once you account for the reranker already sitting on top, and the large ones (ReasonEmbed-4B, depth 100) cost 2–5× the weekly quota. The price is also knowable: your reranker is **prefill-bound at ~490 tokens/s on T4×2 because FlashAttention needs sm≥80** ([ledger](../results/ledger.csv)), so cost scales with passages read, and 1b test (~1,214 steps) costs about **10.5 wall-hours on T4×2**, up to 21 GPU-h if T4×2 bills at 2×. The v2 build order below is: free rank interpolation first, then 1b step reranking as the headline, then a one-session GroupRank throughput gate, then temporal views as pool expansion, then depth only if budget remains. Each step is tested on train with paired bootstrap CIs and a stop/go rule decided before it runs, and dev gets exactly one look at the end.

## Reranking is the only +0.05 lever, and 1b has never used it

Your own measurements rank the stages clearly. Dense retrieval took 1a from BM25's 0.094 to 0.257. Step fusion added +0.0115 on train, **CI [+0.0071, +0.0162]**, and the mechanism is pool union, not score blending: parent weight 0.001 captures the whole gain, and the operator does not matter ([ledger](../results/ledger.csv)). ReasonRank-7B over the fused top-30 then added **+0.0508 [+0.0362, +0.0664]** on 896 train queries and **+0.0673 [+0.0488, +0.0868]** on dev. It won 390 queries and lost 194, and it helped 12 of 13 domains on train, including History (0.3142 → 0.3539 on 413 queries), which carries about two-thirds of the train query-macro ([ledger](../results/ledger.csv)). The reranker earns this because the fused pool holds far more gold than the top-10 shows: **fused recall climbs from 0.345 at @10 to 0.499 at @30**, and the reranker turns that gap into ranking gains.

1b has the same structure and no reranker. The plain step run scores **0.2792 on train and 0.3147 on dev**, and Phase 5c showed that fusing the parent's dense pool into step runs hurts monotonically, down to −0.0083 at w=1 ([ledger](../results/ledger.csv)). So 1b has no cheap pool lever left, and reordering within the pool is its only move. Published evidence points the same way. A stage-aware decomposition study found that sub-queries **cost 1.6–4.3 nDCG@10 at first-stage retrieval but gain 3.9–7.6 at reranking**, with Qwen3-Reranker-8B at +5.9 ([arXiv 2606.08577](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08577)). TEMPO's step-wise results also show a step needs its parent query as context: **DiVeR Query+Step 33.3 vs Step-Only 20.1** on step-level nDCG@10 ([TEMPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09523)). That settles the prompt: give the reranker the same Query+Step string your step retrieval already uses.

The main risk is specific and measurable. A quick check of IOTA train shows every step's gold set is a strict subset of its parent query's gold: **16/16 steps, with 1–2 gold per step against 3–6 per query** (own check, one domain; verify across all 13, which costs nothing). With the parent query in the prompt, the reranker may promote documents that answer a sibling step, and step qrels count those as non-relevant. The experiment below tracks the share of top-10 slots taken by sibling-step gold for exactly this reason. Expected gain on 1b is **+0.03 to +0.06**. This is an inference from your 1a measurement and the stage-aware study, not a published 1b number. It shrinks toward the bottom of that range if step runs have a smaller recall@30 − recall@10 gap than the fused 1a pool's 0.154, which is free to measure first.

## On a T4, prompt tokens set the price, not the model

Every reranker decision hinges on one ledger fact. The Phase 7 run averaged **6.5k prompt tokens and 748 output tokens per window, at 29.7 s/query**, prefill-bound at ~490 tok/s because FlashAttention is unavailable on sm75 ([ledger](../results/ledger.csv)). Two windows (window 20, stride 10) mean ~13k prompt tokens per query, which is ~26.5 s of prefill alone. Cost is therefore close to linear in passages read, and that changes how to read the published speed claims.

GroupRank-7B is the most attractive drop-in on paper. It is Apache-2.0 with a Qwen2.5-7B base, and it outputs continuous scores, which allow real score interpolation. In its own setup over Diver-Retriever-4B top-100 it scores **34.3 vs ReasonRank-7B's 32.5 at 3.4 s vs 28.7 s per query** ([GroupRank](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11653)). But the HF card reports 36.65 for the same 7B model under an unclear setting ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/AQ-MedAI/Diver-GroupRank-7B)), the card specifies BF16, which T4 lacks, and the 8.4× speedup was measured on hardware where decode and window overlap dominate. On your prefill-bound T4, a reranker that reads each of the top-30 passages once instead of 40 passage-reads across two overlapping windows saves **at most ~25% of prefill** (derived, not measured). Expect ~1.3×, not 8×, unless GroupRank's prompts are much shorter. That makes GroupRank a one-session throughput test, not a plan.

Depth follows the same arithmetic. Top-50 needs 4 windows, which doubles cost to ~60–66 s/query. In ReasonRank's own paper, going from top-20 to top-100 (1 → 9 windows) gains **+2.5 nDCG@10 (33.23 → 35.74)** on BRIGHT ([ReasonRank](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07050)), about 0.3 points per extra window. Your fused recall rises only **+0.065 from @30 to @50**, so depth 50 is plausibly worth +0.005 to +0.015 at twice the price. The obvious cost cuts are weaker than they look. Dropping ReasonRank's reasoning mode costs **2.1–2.8 BRIGHT points (35.74 → 32.96/33.60)** and only saves decode, which is the minority of your time ([ReasonRank](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07050)). Cutting passages from 512 to 256 tokens saves less than half, because 6.5k tokens over 20 passages already means ~325 tokens per passage. TEMPO documents also average 370–1,040 tokens ([TEMPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09523)), so temporal cues late in a document get cut. MERIT-Rank claims ReasonRank-level quality in 3 windows instead of 9, but its weight release is unconfirmed ([arXiv 2609.20131](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20131)).

Two patterns explain why the 1a column is so flat. First, **gains shrink as the stage below improves.** View fusion helps weak backbones most, Contriever 19.6 → 25.9 but E5 only 27.1 → 30.3 ([2608.08940](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08940)). Its measured per-query gain on SFR is **+0.013 [+0.006, +0.020]** because the macro gain concentrates in small domains ([2608.08940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08940)), and your leaderboard is macro over queries, dominated by History. Diver is also the one retriever TEMPO found insensitive to added reasoning text (**30.7–32.2 vs 32.0**) ([TEMPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09523)). Keeping views as separate retrievals avoids the concatenation harm documented elsewhere: with separate encoding, AnchorQE beats baseline in 44 of 45 cells, including Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, Diver-0.6B's backbone family ([AnchorQE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25521)). So views are safe, but after reranking they only pay for new gold pushed into the top-30. Your step union moved recall@100 by +0.037 and final nDCG by about one point, and views can be expected to do less.

Second, **the retriever upgrades are priced for a different benchmark.** BRIGHT order does not carry to TEMPO: general 7B embedders E5 (30.4) and SFR (30.0) sit within two points of DiVeR (32.0) there ([TEMPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09523)), and nobody has published ReasonEmbed on TEMPO or a dense+dense pool union on either benchmark. Temporal-specific methods fail for a structural reason: time-aware retrievers such as TempRetriever and Q-TIE match query dates to document timestamps ([TempRetriever](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21024); [Q-TIE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23880)), but the RETECO corpus is `{id, content}` only ([HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/DataScience-UIBK/RETECO-SemEval2027)), and only about 27% of sampled documents contain any year (own check on 6,004 cached documents). TEMPO's hardest class, Trends & Cross-Period (154 queries, DiVeR 23.9) ([TEMPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09523)), is too small a share of the query macro to justify a dedicated method before 1b is reranked.

## Build order for v2 and the tests that gate it

All selection happens on train, and every comparison is paired on an identical query set. The primary metric is **query-macro nDCG@10** for 1a and the **official 1b aggregation** (steps averaged per query, then across queries) for 1b. Uncertainty is a **paired bootstrap over queries: 10,000 resamples, 95% percentile CI of the mean per-query Δ**, seed fixed. Every row also reports win/loss/tie, the domain-macro, and History alone. Reranking steps use the **896-query seeded train subset S** that v1 already reranked, so v1 reranks are reused as the control at zero cost. Where a step tunes anything, selection happens inside the existing 5-fold domain-stratified folds and the reported Δ is out-of-fold. Dev is opened **once**, on the frozen v2.

The order follows value per GPU-hour, and it front-loads the cheap decisions that shape the rest. Step 1 is free and protects against the reranker's known losses: bitcoin fell on train, and iota and monero fell on dev ([ledger](../results/ledger.csv)), consistent with reports of listwise reranking hurting some BRIGHT subsets ([2509.02558](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.02558)). Its recall curve also predicts step 2's payoff before any GPU time is spent. Step 2 is the only step with an expected gain larger than its CI width. Step 3 sits before views because it decides whether depth and full-train reranking are affordable at all, and it can kill itself in about 30 minutes. Step 4's two gates keep a pool lever from spending reranker hours unless recall@30, the reranker's ceiling, actually moves. Depth comes last because it doubles the cost of everything above it.

The test-time bill for the full v2 is about **4.5 h for 1a plus 10.5 h for 1b, or ~15 wall-hours on T4×2**, plus under an hour of view generation for ~519 test queries if step 4 passes. That is two 9-hour sessions and one week of quota at the 2× billing upper bound, and it fits inside the 10–31 January window with a spare week for a fallback run. Training-side validation for steps 1–5 totals roughly **15–20 wall-hours**, or 1–1.5 weeks of quota. That leaves room for one repeat if a Kaggle session dies, which your per-domain checkpointing already handles.

## Conclusion

The v1 results change where the points are. Retrieval-side ideas answer "how do we get more gold into the pool", but your measurements show that the pool already holds far more gold than the top-10 uses, and that one stage (the reranker) converts it. So the best v2 move is not a new model or a new query trick. It is running the existing reranker on the sub-track that has never had one. That comes with a fixed price you can plan around, because on a T4 without FlashAttention cost is simply passages read multiplied by ~31 s per 30. The same arithmetic explains why GroupRank's advertised 8× speedup, ReasonEmbed's BRIGHT lead and the depth curve all look worse on your hardware than in their papers.

The paper also gains from this. A measured 1b reranking gain with the sibling-step diagnostic would be the first published evidence on whether step-level reasoning rerankers confuse a step's evidence with its siblings'. TEMPO's step gold is a partition of the query gold, which makes it an unusually clean testbed for that question. Negative results in steps 3–5 are also worth a line each, because each one tests a published claim (GroupRank speed, view fusion on a reasoning retriever, depth returns) under hardware constraints most participants share.
